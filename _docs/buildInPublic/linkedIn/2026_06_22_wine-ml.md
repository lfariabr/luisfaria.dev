# LinkedIn Post — Wine Data ML Project

**Date:** 2026-06-22

Can metrics define the quality of a wine? Partially yes, according to my latest ML project.

Kicking off my 4th term at my Masters’ journey at Torrens University Australia, I’ve been challenged to work on a regression analysis on an interesting wine dataset straight from the prestigious public research UC Irvine.

𝗤𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗶𝘁𝘆 is a subjective and ordinal score, so I treated every model as a decision-support, not a replacement for expert tasting and to stay structured and iterative, I wrapped the work in CRISP-DM keeping the strategy aligned with actionable results.
- 𝙑𝙖𝙧𝙞𝙖𝙗𝙡𝙚𝙨: quality (the target I trained the models to predict), plus density, pH, sulphates, alcohol, and more.
- 𝘼𝙡𝙜𝙤𝙧𝙞𝙩𝙝𝙢𝙨: Linear regression (baseline), then Ridge, Lasso, ElasticNet and Random Forest, tuned with GridSearchCV (5-fold cross-validation).

The verdict? 
The chemistry explains only 𝗮𝗯𝗼𝘂𝘁 𝗵𝗮𝗹𝗳 of what makes a wine score well.

My best model (random forest, an ensemble of decision trees) reaches 𝗥² ≈ 0.50 and is off by ~0.44 of a quality point on average. The other half is human and sensory - the stuff a lab simply cannot bottle. And because random forest beat the linear models, I know the relationship is non-linear: quality doesn’t move in a straight line with alcohol or acidity.
